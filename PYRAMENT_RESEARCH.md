# Pyrament follow-up — 9 October 2026

Research checkpoint, not a newly implemented public formulation or validated mix.
Owner asked to examine Pyrament and a newer/better alternative, prioritizing
potassium silicate and high strength. Existing five literature designs remain intact.

## Historical baseline

[Davidovits/Sawyer US4509985A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4509985A/en),
Example II: reported 7140 psi (49.2 MPa) on 2-inch mortar cubes after four hours
at 150°F (65.6°C); 5575 psi (38.4 MPa) after 24 hours at 73°F is a separate
condition. Do not relabel heat-cured results as ambient four-hour performance.
The patent's geopolymer/slag examples are not proof of the exact commercial
Pyrament blended-cement formulation. Original patent and marketed product must
remain distinct records.

## Later potassium dry-blend reference

[Davidovits US5349118A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5349118A/en),
Example 6 (1994): mass parts 27 reactive aluminosilicate oxide, 21 slag,
15 silica dust, 19 technical potassium disilicate powder, 215 normalized sand,
21.5 added water. Reported setting begins at 35 minutes; 16 MPa after four
hours at 20°C. The powder refers to Example 1, with approximately 14% water;
it is not interchangeable by mass with aqueous potassium silicate or an
arbitrary anhydrous powder. Preserve added water separately from powder water.
No 28-day result is extracted for this example. This is a patent claim, not
independent reproduction or the original commercial Pyrament recipe.

## Current performance comparator

[CTS Cement All Plus datasheet](https://www.ctscement.com/datasheet/CEMENT_ALL_PLUS_Datasheet_DS_231_EN_13?c=PRODUCTS&t=resources%2Ftech-bulletins%2Fwater-curing%2F),
checked 9 October 2026: manufacturer reports 27.6 MPa at three hours and
55.2 MPa at 28 days, ASTM C109 modified, at 21°C and 3.7 quarts water per
55 lb bag. Proprietary hydraulic repair mortar, not a potassium-silicate
geopolymer recipe. Different tests/materials prohibit a direct superiority claim.

## Recommended next experiment direction (proposal)

Keep two objectives separate: high early strength at ambient temperature and
high 28-day strength. Neither guarantees the other. Investigate metakaolin/slag
with characterized potassium silicate as the potassium-focused development
track; use the historical dry-blend example as a reference, not an assumed
equivalent for liquid KASIL substitutions. Obtain activator solids/modulus,
precursor assays and particle-size data before calculating a supplier adaptation.

Compare working time, four-hour/one-day/28-day compression under the same test
method and temperature, then flexure, shrinkage and substrate bond if repair is
the target. For studio panels, prioritize thin-section cracking and working time
alongside strength. These are proposed measurements, not predicted outcomes.

No newer potassium-silicate formulation was verified here as definitively
superior to Pyrament. Next public addition should be a clearly labeled historical
reference after source-table/ingredient-basis review, followed by separately
identified experimental supplier adaptations. Do not publish inferred performance.
